04 — Before you start

A few things
you’ll need.

A compatible Mac, an Android tablet and a data cable. This is an experimental preview with a deliberately narrow tested setup.

On your Mac
Apple Silicon · macOS 13+arm64 only; no Intel build. macOS 13 is the declared minimum, while older versions than the tested release remain unverified.
On your tablet
Android 8.1 or newerHardware H.264 decoding, USB debugging and the matching UScreen receiver APK are required.
Between the two
A USB data cable + adbCharge-only cables won’t work. Android platform-tools must be installed on the Mac. Wi-Fi transport is not supported.

Start with the installation guide.

It walks through both apps, USB authorization, setup and the Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions for the installed backend. Installing the app alone does not authorize a display session.

Tested on one setup. M1 Pro, macOS 26.5.1 and DOOGEE U12 over USB, at 1600×960 and 2000×1200. Other hardware and modes are unverified; this is not a general stability claim.

Know the limits

The app is signed locally and not notarized; first launch needs an app-specific macOS override. Keep Gatekeeper and SIP enabled. Display creation uses Apple’s unsupported CGVirtualDisplay interface, so a future macOS update could break it. Read the known limitations before installing.

05 — Under the hood

Native on the Mac.
Local over USB.

Video goes to the tablet.
Touch comes back to the Mac.

Local transport & privacy

Video and input use loopback sockets tunneled through adb reverse to the attached tablet. Session tokens authenticate the connection, and each installation is bound to one device. No listener on your local network, cloud account, analytics or telemetry is involved in the display connection.

The independent supervisor can stop a session when safety limits are reached. Local logs can contain device identity, window titles and timings; these controls prevent accidental starts and are not a security boundary against a local administrator.

Privacy and local data flows

Performance & hardware evidence

On the published preview.3 packages, an M1 Pro with macOS 26.5.1 and a DOOGEE U12 at 1600×960 captured 28.8–43.0 fps while a window moved; the tablet acknowledged 28.0–42.8 fps. Tap and touch-drag were operator-confirmed. These are measurements on one setup, not a performance promise.

A 60 Hz display setting does not mean 60 fps. Capture follows screen changes, an idle desktop produces almost no frames, and the tablet decoder affects latency. Synthetic benchmarks and normal use have different conditions.

Recommended release: measurements and acceptance evidence

Platform, input & everyday limitations

One tablet at a time. Resolution changes require a host restart. Video is H.264 and SDR, with no HEVC or HDR support. Touch covers click, drag and two-finger scroll; pen pressure, tilt, eraser and pinch-to-zoom are not implemented.

Long sessions can stress the Mac graphics stack. The supervisor monitors the session and may stop it. The recommended build can occasionally enter quarantine after Disconnect and require operator recovery; see troubleshooting before reconnecting.

The receiver is signed with the project’s own key. An upstream Android receiver with a different signature must be uninstalled before installing this one. There is no auto-update.

UScreen handles the display; it does not execute agents, host models, schedule workers, synchronize projects or provide an SSH server.
